# The Grove - Labyrinth Puzzle Dungeon

The goal is to provide satisfying, engaging  solutions to classic challenges...

They cannot be unsolvable, or completely real-world based.

The characters need to be able to roll for "tips" or hints, if they don't want to use IRL smarts/cleverness. (And with a high enough/right roll, the direct answer)

There should still be the opportunity for combat, or encounters, that maybe play into the puzzle aspect of things.

- Ex. Solo Leveling's Double Dungeon is an example of a "combat puzzle encounter" with a god-tier boss
  - can we do something similar, but with lower level mobs?
- Is there something aBOUT the room that is uncovered through spilt blood or movement during combat, etc.

## Aesthetics and Environment

The grove is a natural, intimate, organic stage at Sham. How does that translate to the experience of a Labyrinth? Is it just hard to find, tucked away in the forest?

    "Home Of The Gnomies"

Expose from Sham website:

> Deep in the Shambhala forest lies a special place that has quietly evolved over the years. A welcoming dance floor surrounded by towering cedar trees, lush pathways, creative installations and cool forest lounge areas. The Grove is an invitation to immerse yourself in a fully interactive environment that celebrates music and dance amongst BC’s natural setting. The organic stage orientation provides a beautiful backdrop to it’s renowned music line up and theatrical performance art. After sunset, The Grove transforms with a full spectrum Funktion-one sound system and state of the art lighting designed to enliven and free the senses. Alongside the dance floors – you will undoubtedly be drawn to the Grove Art Gallery, Tea Hive and Secret Garden.

A grove is a place of trees and no shrubbery or underbrush, more like an orchard... Even better if it's an oasis in the desert dunes, ala Gerudo Desert & the Spirit Temple.
I do like the ancient archeological site vibe, with etched inscriptions, grooves and carvings, stonework covered in moss or vines desperate for water and shelter from the sun above.
The "Grove" as a dungeon could be essentially concentric rings, or non-euclidean space, and each deeper level is more expansive, reveals more of the archeology, all while actually affecting the world outside the entrance. Unlocking or progressing within the dungeon actually reflects in the outside world, by revealing the "grove" and the ruins have "entered" the Prime plane, right outside the entrance.

- (First, it's just a handful of trees and an oasis,
- then it's a ring of trees, around a sizeable lake amid the desert,
- then it's a large belt of trees surrounding a larger lake with ruins and aqueducts and stonework jutting out from the water, within the treeline, etc.
- Finally, they  either enter and proceed to the center)

Leaning into the puzzle, the "oasis", etc - having the players have to dive into the water, in order to even "arrive" at the dungeon is great - and similarly, getting them to leave by swimming back up, and seeing how the pool has changed once they arrive, is PERFECT for the disorienting and confuddling feelings - just like a labyrinth should be.

When completing the dungeon, receiving the treasure, and leaving (assuming NOT forcibly removed for timing out) - heroes will be in the "reward chamber" - which looks like the above described Grove, whether or not it's the same as when they first found it, or a result of going "deeper" and it getting "bigger on the inside", when they leave through the same door/passage/etc. they entered said Rewards chamber through, it should eventually shift and evolve into the same passage they took to access the ACTUAL DUNGEON - the entrance. And as they leave the place, or they walk back out into the Prime material plane, everything looks the same as when they arrived, but - looking around, you notice the landscape has changed. THAT SAME reward chamber (minus the rewards, switches, specific ruin iconography, puzzles, or other critical McGuffins - which are all eroded/collapsed/otherwise ruined) has in fact appeared just outside the "entrance" you just left. Yet there is no mistake - the sky, the world, the earth you stand on is absolutely "back on Faerûn".

## Logistics and Plans

Dungeon should be suitable for 4 sessions, and the idea of it being progressive is appealing... As each enters, the state of the place is preserved, so doors that were unlocked or closed remain so for the next faction. Ideally, even shared time space... Where the Labyrinth doesn't exist unless someone is inside, so if another team just finished a campfire and short rest, the next faction will find the fire warm or even still lit.
Progression should be 1 of 2 ways, either :

- each group enters where the last group completed, so a freshly opened gate rewards both a key on the pedestal beyond, but also access deeper into the dungeon.
- A single antechamber, where success is defined by "flipping all the switches", and after each one is lit, it also opens a small hidden box in the wall, where they Key resides.
  - In addition - lighting up all the lights, may result in a different reward, encounter, or the final faction actually has a combat-based-puzzle encounter.

Let's work with the Progressive, Oasis, Archeological ruins vibe.

## The Grove — Four-Year Encounter Summary

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | **Fivefold Crossing** | **Cycle** — *"Every ending returns to its beginning."* | Guide a hostile construct through a cyclic sequence of pressure plates. Completing the sequence requires the first and final states to coexist rather than simply killing the construct. | **The Endless Ring** | A rotating turquoise-and-gold ring with no true beginning, end, top, or bottom. It eventually becomes the outer frame of the completed device. |
| **II** | **Bloody Floor** | **Connection** — *"What appears separate may belong to one design."* | Blood spilled during combat reveals that seemingly unrelated grooves across the chamber form a single hidden sigil. | **III** | **Unending Guardians** | **Restoration** — *"What is broken may still have purpose."* | Overgrown Barkwood Elementals endlessly regenerate until their missing pieces are recovered and returned. ## The Four Laws of the Grove

1. **Cycle** — Every ending returns to its beginning.
2. **Connection** — What appears separate may belong to one design.
3. **Restoration** — What is broken may still have purpose.
4. **Perspective** — What is hidden from one side may be revealed from another.

### Final Principle: Convergence

> **The rules were never separate.**

Each annual encounter teaches one fundamental property of the Grove. Individually, the artifacts appear mysterious, incomplete, or impossible. Together, they reveal that the puzzles were never arbitrary trials — they were demonstrations of how the Grove itself functions.

**Artifact Assembly:**  
`Endless Ring → Conduits → Anchor → Impossible Mirror → Four-Year Gate`

### Encounters - Fivefold Crossing

This is a Construct fight + Sequence puzzle.

The closed chamber contains a sequence of large pressure plates—perhaps representing phases of the moon, movement of the sun, seasons, or another cyclical riddle/Sphinx/Nature/Rebirth motif. The entrance seals behind them as roots and trees grow to bar the way back; A massive construct animates from the surrounding ruins, and attacks the party.

The key mechanic are the plates scattered throughout the room.

Each plate requires either:

- the combined weight of the party, or
- the weight of the construct alone.

The players gradually realize they're supposed to manipulate the construct's movement rather than simply kill it.

The sequence is cyclical. Given the "Moon" example:

- New → Crescent → Half → Gibbous → Full → New

The critical realization is that completing a cycle requires the final **and** first positions to be active simultaneously.
That creates the finale we want: the party collectively occupies one plate while baiting/shoving/directing the construct onto another — or finally kills the construct atop the required plate, leaving its enormous body permanently depressing it.

A physical tabletop device can represent the sequence with illuminated buttons/plates, giving players something tangible to experiment with while the fictional encounter unfolds.

Typical setback is baiting the enemy in the wrong direction, or the wrong person taking agro and pulling onto the wrong pressure plate, which resets or rewinds one step in the sequence.

Theme: Control rather than destruction.

### Encounter - Bloody Floor

This is an onslaught combat + missing text / geometry puzzle. (classic, backup puzzle)

Keeping this deliberately straightforward.
A combat begins over a floor covered in decorative grooves. (this can be a riddle, with lines missing - a mystical sigil, to unlock the door, etc.)

Defeating an enemy - Blood spills- It doesn't pool. It flows.

The blood follows carved channels, illuminating previously invisible geometry, text, riddles, etc.
From there, players realize creatures need to bleed/die in particular places to complete the sigil, learn the code to unlock the door, etc.

Physical prop can be revealed by color(like a red lens) or simply drawn in on paper with red pen, connecting the right lines to form words and riddles in the area where an enemy perished.

Theme: Observation changes understanding.

### Encounter - Unending Guardians

Several ancient guardians attack. Undead, or living plants, etc.

Defeating them normally accomplishes nothing.
Their bodies collapse.
A moment later:
Stone pulls itself together. Armor reforms. New branches sprout into limbs - and the guardian rises.

But environmental clues show statues/alcoves depicting those same guardians/armor/etc in an earlier state.
Perhaps each originally possessed:
Sword — Shield — Mace — Lantern
When defeated, the items can be retrieved and returned to the right alcove. In the correct location, the guardian / armor doesn't regenerate.

Instead:
The shattered stone and armor draws backward into the alcove. Cracks close. For the first time, you realize you haven't destroyed the creature...
You've restored the statue.

Eventually all guardians stand motionless where they belonged thousands of years ago.
They "move" once more, either settling like switches depressed, or by crossing armaments.
And the way opens.

Theme: Restoration rather than destruction.

### Encounter - Echo Chamber

The chamber itself is a signature location, but also the site of Encounter #3.
Every expedition passes through it, right after entering the grove, this is the next room you will encounter, and be *unable* to solve early on.

At first it seems almost mundane by Grove standards: a circular chamber, perhaps 15 or 20 similar mechanisms littered around its walls, and a perfectly still, shallow reflecting pool at its center. Then something strange happens. A lever moves by itself. Then another. Then two at once, at opposing ends of the room. Yet - nothing is visible. Those keen eyed may see movement, or something, beneath the ever-still waters of the pool at the center of the room.

Eventually, someone may interact with the pool — possibly looking beneath its surface. And suddenly they can see them: Echoes. Shadowy figures occupy the reflection of this room on the other side of the water. They're the other parties, but from years ago, and muddled like smoke or shadow, not fully realized. Still, their features are recognizable: a distinct build, a silhouette, iconic parts of armor or weapons - noticeable and familiar *enough*.

The water is a reflection of space AND time. And, submerging their head doesn't feel like diving underwater - you are able to breathe normally, and perceive this "past" chamber around while your body remains in the present. (the pool isn't deep enough to lay in or dive into) (major Pensieve from HP vibes).

Requires notation and description on part of the DM, to know exactly what happened each year and how they intersect. But it creates an ideal & phenomenal recurring mechanic, enabling teamwork even across sessions.

Year 1
Players discover the echo chamber, the only echo's they may see are from shades unfamiliar to them, and who don't really interact with the room or show how it works. For this party, the room remains a mystery.
They may intentionally manipulate certain mechanisms still, trying to solve the puzzle at first - You record exactly what they do. if too much time spent on it, DM can urge the party to move on, or pressure other ways.

Year 2
Those actions occur within the room again. Except now, anyone peering into the pool can see and watch their Year 1 counterparts.
And perhaps something Year 1 team did, suddenly becomes useful.
Meanwhile, Year 2 establishes actions that Year 3 will inherit.

Year 3
Now multiple temporal layers could exist, and intermingle, or phase through and walk into each other, but the actions all happen from the same starting time, whenever the party crosses the threshold that leads into the chamber.

The players finally understand:
What we do here matters in the future.

So for the first time, they deliberately leave something behind for their future selves.

It's possible to end it here with something like 15 different levers, or continue to year 4 with [20].
Still - The impossible configuration requires more simultaneous actions than five adventurers could ever perform. But they're not just a team of five They have 3-4 expeditions worth, all working together, across past and into the present. That is when the twenty levers suddenly make sense.

ALSO - if anyone dips their head into the pool in Year 1... From Year 2 onwards, that "Echo" will at some point approach the pool, and puts their head through it, just like the past-selves did. That should visually occur and be noticed by the party next year - a serve as a bit of an obvious call to investigate, but again, it's there just in case they get lost or wander beyond too soon.

Theme: Perspective / Memory / Past enables future.

### Optional Encounter Puzzle - The Four-Year Gate — Finale

Still intentionally under-designed.

Current requirements:

- Each expedition produces a physical Grove artifact.
- Each artifact should make sense independently.
- Each represents something learned through that expedition.

The artifacts initially appear to be rewards, keys, curiosities, or pieces of Grove technology.
At the end, the party discovers that they're components of something larger.
Even better if represented by a physical payoff here too, where pieces fit together or connect somehow.

Reward connection between teams, and providing loot despite the competition, that unlocks something powerful and defining ahead of the Epic dungeon.

Consider an outcropping with a circular keyhole, but you've only collected an ancient ring... And another faction has a smaller ring... That actually fits inside **YOUR** ring... and if you collect all 4, another smaller ring and a small disc, they connect together, and align with the grooves on the keyhole, and open a vault to unique artifacts with major stat boons you can keep, trade, or barter away!

BONUS points if we can somehow work previous puzzle themes or aspects into the shapes of these overlapping keys, so the "teachings" of the Grove persist beyond the encounter, or are represented by the trinkets themselves.

Themes as it stands at the moment:

The overall progression

- Year I — CYCLE: Understand repetition.
- Year II — CONNECTION: See relationships between apparently separate things.
- Year III — RESTORATION: Recognize that apparent destruction/incompletion can conceal original purpose.
- Year IV — PERSPECTIVE: Change how you observe reality to perceive what was always there.
- Finale — CONVERGENCE: Realize these aren't four puzzle lessons—they are four fundamental properties of the Grove itself.

Artifacts as rewards / key for extra loot later:

- Ring: looks strange, but physically normal.
- Conduits: behave impossibly only when combined with the Ring.
- Anchor: establishes that the assembly has a specific orientation and structure.
- Mirror: behaves impossibly even by itself—and then allows solid matter to occupy impossible space within it.
- Completed Device: finally makes the previous impossibilities appear intentional rather than random.
