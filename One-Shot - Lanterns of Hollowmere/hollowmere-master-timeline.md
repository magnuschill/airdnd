# The Lantern at Hollowmere — DM Master Timeline

*A scene-by-scene story-beat reference for a gothic D&D 5e one-shot.*

> **Prep scope:** Story flow, revelations, and player choices only. Dungeon layouts, encounter mechanics, stat blocks, specific DCs, and dialogue remain to be designed.
>
> **Structure:** Arrival evening (Night 0) → Day 1 → Day 2 → Day 3 → Night 3 → Finale. Scenes may be combined, reordered within a day, or shortened as play demands. **Critical clues should reach the PC through events or conversations, not depend on successful rolls.**

## Chapter I — Arrival in Hollowmere
*First evening (Night 0) | Establish the town, Wren, and the haunting.*

### 01. The Bridge into Hollowmere
- **Present:** Wren Cade, PC, accompanying party.
- The party arrives at dusk, three days after Wren's letter (they wrote back that they would come). He meets them on the stone bridge at the edge of the fog-choked river town.
- Wren repeats the letter's problems in person: the lamps failing late at night, townspeople walking in their sleep, Constable Dench doing little, the Lightmaster saying to wait it out.
- Wren insists they **keep a flame on them after dark**. He can't say why, only that it matters.
- **Wren withholds:** His sister Isla's dependence on the Draught and his own history of taking it.
- The walk through the quiet lanes takes a while; it is well past closing when they reach the inn. Wren spoke with Bree earlier today and asked her to wait up for them.
- **Lead:** Follow Wren to the Gutterwick Inn.

### 02. The Gutterwick Inn
- **Present:** Mother Bree Goodbarrel and Wren only. The inn is effectively closed; Bree stayed up and lets Wren in because he asked her earlier that day. Tamsin Reed is off-shift tonight (she is at the inn on Night 1). Constable Dench is not a regular here.
- **Bree:** Gets the party settled with lodging, a late bowl of something warm, and a few quiet rumors.
- **The notice:** The Lamplighters' notice is posted on the door (moved up from Day 1). Bree points it out as she bars the door behind them: *do not wake anyone you find walking in their sleep.*
- **Wren:** Explains the town's belief that the vigil lamps keep the Quiet at bay. This is folk wisdom; he does **not** link it to the fog.
- **Clues:** Locals blame the ghost for the extinguished lamps. Bree mentions that the fog has lately smelled strangely sweet.

### 03. A Figure in the Fog *(brief optional stinger)*
- From the inn window or a nearby lane, the PC glimpses a pale, short, broad-shouldered silhouette (a dwarven woman). A lamp gutters out as the figure passes, and a faint wail carries on the wind.
- She does not speak. Do not force a confrontation.
- **Purpose:** Make the ghost theory convincing without confirming it.

## Chapter II — The Failing Lights
*Day 1 | Establish the pattern of failures and the Draught connection.*

### 04. The Morning Streets and the Lamplighters' Hall
- **Present:** Wren, townsfolk, Fonkin Pennywhistle (the Lightmaster).
- Wren shows the PC recently failed lamps and relays reports of people walking at night without awareness.
- At the Hall, Fonkin freely hands over the lamp-failure ledger. He is arrogantly convinced it is a maintenance problem (bad oil, sloppy apprentices) and wants everyone to wait for the next oil shipment. He does not believe in ghosts, and he is wrong.
- **Key clue (the ledger):** The failed lamps trace a wandering path across town that matches where the pale figure has been seen. This is the red herring, and it is why the town blames her. It also shows *who* is affected: people living along her route.
- Fonkin dismisses the pattern as coincidence and sticks to his oil theory.

### 05. The People Who Walk
- **Present:** Wren, Bree/Tamsin, witnesses; can be folded into breakfast or the Hall scene.
- Witnesses describe sleepwalkers drifting silently out of their homes. **Where they end up is the key detail:** they gather near the sewer grates and around the Mill, as close to the sweet smell as they can get, even a mile from their own doors. Any named NPC the PC asks (Bree, Tamsin, Wren, Fonkin, townsfolk) can answer "but where do they go?" clearly. Many were ill before, or recently.
- **How it works (DM):** Kathra's cold snuffs lamps along her route. The mist then creeps into those streets, and Draught users there catch the scent and rise. Once walking, they drift toward the strongest source of the scent: the drains and the Mill.
- **Rumor:** Many think the walkers are being led to their deaths by the ghost.
- **Key clue:** **Draught** users recur among those affected. Not every user walks, and not every walker used it, but there are too many for coincidence. The medicine is a popular cure-all, especially after the recent plague season.
- Wren appears uneasy when Isla or the Draught comes up; he need not confess yet.
- **Lead:** Ashbury's Apothecary, the public face of the Draught. It is closed today ("opens tomorrow").

### 06. A Quiet Night *(Night 1; short rest/roleplay beat)*
- Allow the PC to review clues and speak privately with Wren. No major threat is required. A chance for more information from the innfolk.
- **Tamsin:** Has seen unsettling movement in the streets on late shifts and can discuss reported sleepwalkers.
- **The ghost:** Nobody knows who she is. She appeared sometime after last season's plague, when many died and many were buried quickly. Some say she brought the plague; others say she walks for the dead. The Quiet is older than the ghost (a little has always existed), but it got much worse after she appeared, which is why the town blames her.
- **The warden:** Bree or Tamsin mentions the abbey's old warden, a hermit who died with the rest in the plague. In her later years she kept to a cottage near the abbey, came into town only for food, and said little. Nobody knew what she did up there, nobody ever learned her name, and the cottage has stood empty since. No one connects her to the ghost. This is the lead to the cottage.
- **Dench:** Not a regular at the inn. If sought out (or met around Cinder Row), he knows more than he admits, but dismisses the reports and refuses to act without proof.
- **By day's end:** The PC should know about the failed lamps, the sewer pattern, the sweet smell, the sleepwalkers, and the possible Draught connection.

## Chapter III — The Price of a Cure
*Day 2 | Link the Draught to the Mill; pursue an optional side lead.*

### 07. Ashbury's Apothecary
- **Present:** Dr. Lenore Ashbury, Isla Cade, Wren, party.
- Isla is buying another bottle of the Draught. She leaves as the PC enters; Wren may scold or talk with her outside. (An argument and an opportunity.)
- Ashbury is charming, clean, professional, and defensive under questioning. She explains the Draught's reputation as a cure-all, its closely guarded local recipe, and its popularity during last season's plague.
- **Key clue:** Draught bottles bear **Marrow Mill's mark**. The apothecary is a nearby public retail location, **not the dungeon entrance**.
- **Hidden truth:** Ashbury is enthralled by the Dreamer. Do not reveal this. A subtle tell: a faint sweet scent, the same as the fog.

### 08. Follow a Lead — Cinder Row **or** the Warden's Cottage
*Two optional investigative options, not a requirement to complete both.*

**A. Cinder Row / The Sallow Court**
- **Present:** Phelaia, thieves, possibly Dench looking the other way.
- Phelaia traffics in cut street Draught; negotiation, bribery, or confrontation are all possible.
- The PC may gain information, a sample, or a potential ally among criminals who do not know the whole truth.
- **Geography:** Old drain stairs near **Cinder Row** descend into the sewer network. This becomes the **standard dungeon entry point** on Night 3.

**B. The Warden's Cottage (Kathra's home)**
- A lead for PCs who want to know more about the ghost and the abbey's hermit warden. The cottage stands near the abbey, a short walk out of town.
- A journal describes protective rune wards that required ongoing maintenance. **It is unsigned.** It tells the PC what she did, not who she was.
- The PC learns that the warden tended wards that have since decayed.
- If the PC lingers until dusk, Kathra may appear, wailing and gesturing, without forcing a fight.

### 09. Wren's Burden *(Night 2; can be folded into the return to the inn)*
- Wren's old hip injury flares after days on his feet. He may talk about having once relied on the Draught and his worry for Isla.
- **The injury story (and the entrance):** When Wren recounts how he got hurt, he reveals that he fell from a lamp scaffold right outside the old drain grate and stairs near Cinder Row. He doesn't realize it matters, but the PC now has the dungeon entrance location even if they chose Option B.
- Establish his temptation and concealment, but **do not stage a return visit to the apothecary**.
- **By day's end:** The PC should suspect that the Draught and Marrow Mill matter, and that the abbey's neglected wards may too.

## Chapter IV — What the Wards Kept
*Day 3 | Reveal who the ghost was and prepare the crisis.*

### 10. Greywake Abbey and Cemetery *(late Day 3 / start of Night 3)*
- **Present:** Wren, Rhogar the gravekeeper, party.
- At the ruined abbey, the PC finds a **half-faded rune circle**: the abbey's existing ward, weakened by neglect. Nobody needs to renew it, and the PC can't. It is simply why the Dreamer is stirring.
- **The abbey tunnel:** One old, ordinary tunnel leads from the abbey cellar down into the sewers. It is not magical. Kathra once explored it and will point it out on Night 3.
- **The abbey register:** One name recurs across many years, a regular who tended the grounds and wards, until the entries stop during last season's plague. The hand matches the cottage journal if the PC found it. It shows who she was, not what she became.
- **Rhogar:** The only one who has connected the ghost to a person. He buried her in haste during the plague, without proper rites, and blames himself for her restlessness. That guilt is why he has done nothing; he is afraid to speak her name to her. He gives her name and shows her grave to anyone who asks or spends time with him.
- **Pacing fallback:** If the PC reaches the abbey on Night 3 without the name, Rhogar is there and can supply it.
- **Key clue:** Knowing she tended the wards is not enough. Her **name** is what calms her.
- *(Optional)* Wren's mother's lantern was made with the warden's runework (his mother called the maker only "the Warden"). Kathra reacts to it.

### 11. Wren's Temptation and the Last Quiet Evening
- Wren's pain intensifies. **If he obtains Draught on Day 3**, it is through Cinder Row/street supply, secretly taking some of Isla's supply, or a similar source, **not a second apothecary scene**.
- The PC may notice or intervene; acquiring a vial is not mandatory for the midnight trigger.
- **If he doses:** he is tethered and can be pulled by the mist tonight (a save against it).
- Back at the inn, allow preparation, conversation, and reflection. The party may compare the sewer/fog evidence with the ward mystery.
- **By day's end:** The PC should have enough information to choose how to respond when the town's crisis begins.

## Chapter V — When the Lanterns Die
*Night 3 | The turning point and route selection.*

### 12. The Midnight Procession
- Wren wakes the party at midnight (whether or not he got Draught). Tamsin also notices the procession passing the inn.
- Lamps fail along Kathra's route, sweet mist creeps into those streets, and Draught users there rise and walk, converging on the drains and **the Marrow Mill area**.
- **Important distinction:** Marrow Mill stands above/near the Dreamer's imprisoned chamber and is the focal destination of the sleepwalkers, **not the ordinary entrance to the full dungeon route**.
- The party has two actionable leads: **the old drain stairs near Cinder Row** (Wren's injury story pointed to it, and he can lead) or **Kathra and Greywake Abbey**.
- Kathra walks tonight, wailing. Lamps die where she passes. The PC can meet her at the abbey or intercept her in the lanes.

### 13. Choose the Descent

**Path A — Cinder Row: The Full Dungeon**
- Reach the drain stairs near Cinder Row.
- Traverse dungeon rooms **1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5**, ultimately reaching the chamber beneath/near Marrow Mill.
- This is the straightforward investigative route, but longer and harder.

**Path B — Kathra: The Abbey Tunnel**
- Kathra does not speak. She wails and gestures, and she attacks anyone who approaches armed and without her name. Her cold extinguishes lamps, but she is not the true villain.
- **Speaking her name** calms her and ends the violence.
- **If peacefully resolved:** She gestures toward the abbey tunnel and points the way, entering at **room 4** and bypassing rooms 1–3. She grants her **blessing**: advantage on saves against the Dreamer's charm, and spirit-light that lets the PC's weapons **banish** the Dreamer. She fades to rest, knowing someone will carry on.
- **If destroyed:** Her cold no longer snuffs the lamps, and the party and townsfolk can relight and hold the streets until dawn. Only Kathra knew where the tunnel leads, so the PC must go via Path A, without her blessing.

## Chapter VI — The Dream Beneath the Mill
*Finale | Dungeon specifics are placeholders pending separate design.*

### 14. Room 1 — Fog Drains *(Cinder Row route only)*
- Mist/charm hazards and environmental traps.
- **Knowledge payoff:** Fire and vigil lamps burn back the fog.

### 15. Room 2 — Sallow Court Gate *(Cinder Row route only)*
- Negotiate, bribe, threaten, or fight with Sallow Court members.
- Prior dealings with Phelaia may secure information or allies.
- Otherwise, a fight against two simple brigands.

### 16. Room 3 — Cistern Puzzle *(Cinder Row route only)*
- Old ward-lamps in the cistern are fed by a series of channels.
- Skill checks determine how to route the flow so the lamps relight.
- The relit lamps trip a switch that reveals a hidden door onward.

### 17. Room 4 — Ossuary Stair *(both routes converge)*
- Tethered, enthralled townspeople obstruct passage.
- The accompanying party handles most of the opposition abstractly; the PC has the meaningful spotlight.
- The abbey tunnel route joins here.
- Two party members stay behind to hold the stair; the PC and one other go onward.

### 18. Room 5 — The Dreamer *(final boss)*
- **Location:** Rune-bound chamber underground beneath/near **Marrow Mill**.
- **Present:** The Dreamer, enthralled Dr. Ashbury (on blood duty), possibly other physician thralls.
- **Backup:** The party, or turncoat Sallow Court thieves if Phelaia was won over.
- **Revelation:** The Dreamer's perfumed breath travels through sewers as fog. The physicians have drawn her blood to make the Draught; recent users are magical tethers empowering her escape.
- **Banishing:** Only Kathra's blessing (or a comparable holy or spirit-infused source, at the DM's discretion) can **truly banish** her to the Feywild. Without it she can be defeated, but not gone.
- Exact combat, encounter balance, and the banishing mechanic TBD.

### 19. Epilogue — The First Light
- **False victory:** Kathra is destroyed and the PC stops there. Relit lamps hold and Hollowmere relaxes, but the seals fail about a month later and the Dreamer is freed.
- **Hard victory ("dead but not gone"):** The Dreamer is vanquished via the full underground route without Kathra's blessing. An evil laugh follows the party out of the sewers. The Quiet continues, and she will return.
- **True victory:** Kathra is put to rest and the Dreamer is banished to the Feywild with her blessing. The Quiet truly stops.
- Close with the town's reaction, Wren's promised **warded heirloom lantern**, and advancement to **level 3**.

## DM Guardrails
- **Protect the mystery:** Kathra's lamp-snuffing is a convincing red herring; sewer-borne mist and the Dreamer are the actual danger.
- **Keep the two patterns separate:** Lamp failures follow Kathra's path and decide *who* is affected. Where walkers are *found* (the drains and the Mill) points to the true source.
- **Avoid clue bottlenecks:** Give essential information through events, NPCs, and records. Checks add context, not access to the plot.
- **Preserve agency:** Side investigations can be chosen freely. The PC's decisions, not the larger party, drive the conclusion.
- **Keep geography consistent:** **Cinder Row = standard underground entrance; Greywake Abbey = alternate tunnel; Marrow Mill = location above/near the final chamber; Apothecary = nearby public-facing seller.**
- **Kathra never speaks.** She wails and gestures. Her name is the key, not an explanation.
- **The wards need no repair.** The PC doesn't recharge them; banishing the Dreamer ends the threat.
- **No second apothecary visit:** Wren's potential Day 3 dose comes from the street, Isla's supply, or another believable source.
- **Don't link the fog yet:** The town knows the lamps keep the Quiet at bay. Let the PC be the one to connect it to the fog.
- **Story first:** Puzzle implementation, detailed battles, and room mechanics are intentionally deferred.
